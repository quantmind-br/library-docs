When you run a modern company, you can lose a surprising amount of money through bad data long before you realise that anything is wrong. For example, your staff may spend hours resolving duplicate records, incorrect contact details, or incomplete profiles, which means your company ultimately pays wages for work that leads nowhere. Worse, this kind of problem tends to multiply and worsen quickly if it’s not addressed. For example, when you add new staff or systems on top of messy records, your team will spread the same errors across more work. Here, we’ll take a look at the hidden costs of bad data for modern companies, and how you can sort out your own data hygiene.

Phone records can waste hours of staff time

Your team needs [reliable phone data](https://trestleiq.com/phone-validation-api/) if you want staff to reach real people instead of chasing dead numbers. A rep who reads a wrong digit from the CRM may spend several minutes reaching voicemail or the wrong household. When a support agent calls an outdated number, that agent must hunt through other records before speaking with the right customer. When marketing staff load contact lists into diallers or messaging tools, bad phone records can turn a full calling day into a string of failed attempts. Cleaning phone records before campaigns begin saves your team from repeating the same mistakes all week.

Sales and support teams struggle with poor-quality data

Your sales staff see the cost of messy records during the first call attempt. A rep may dial the same person twice because another employee created a second profile months earlier. A salesperson may read notes from the wrong account and start a call with the wrong details. Those mistakes waste paid time and make the company sound careless. Your support agents also often face the same problem later in the [customer journey](https://www.bynder.com/en/glossary/customer-journey/). For example, when an agent opens a profile with outdated contact details, that agent may delay a refund, miss a callback, or ask a customer to repeat information your company should already have to hand.

Marketing and finance staff also pay the price

Marketing staff depend on accurate contact lists when they prepare campaigns. When your team uploads weak lists into email or advertising platforms, inaccurate data means that your staff could target the wrong people and count responses that do not reflect real customer interest. Finance staff also depend on clean records when they examine revenue numbers. If an employee counts the same customer under two profiles, a manager may misread customer totals and base decisions or [forecasts](https://waybar.org/how-modern-businesses-improve-forecast-accuracy-across-sales-and-operations/) on distorted figures. Remember, bad records usually aren’t confined to a single department. Employees pass the errors along whenever they share data across tools.

Ordinary work habits prevent most data problems

You don’t need a dramatic cleanup project to stop most data problems. You need simple habits that staff follow every day. A manager should check new contact lists before anyone imports them into the CRM. A salesperson should double-check and correct a number or email address while the customer is still on the phone. A support agent should fix obvious errors before closing a ticket. When each person corrects records during normal work, your company will prevent small errors from spreading across several systems

Treat data maintenance as routine work

You should treat data maintenance as part of the working week instead of saving it for a large repair project later. Ideally, managers should review sample customer profiles and correct obvious errors, and staff should merge duplicates while the account history is still open on the screen. Team leaders should decide who owns each part of a customer record so that employees do not assume someone else will fix mistakes later. When people correct records during everyday work, the company can avoid a slow buildup of small errors that quietly drain time and money.
