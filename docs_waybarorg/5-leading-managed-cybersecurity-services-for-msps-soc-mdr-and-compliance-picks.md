## How we narrowed the field

Before we crowned any winners, we mapped the terrain. More than 600 companies now claim to offer some flavor of MDR or SOC-as-a-Service, far too many for a busy MSP to vet one by one. So we built a filter that mirrors the day-to-day realities of running a service desk.

First, we insisted on round-the-clock human monitoring. Anything less leaves a gap attackers can exploit at 2 am.

Second, we looked for a proven partner program. That means multi-tenant dashboards, white-label options, and real training—features your technicians and account managers will touch every week.

Third came integration depth. If a provider can’t plug into mainstream RMMs, PSAs, and Microsoft 365 logs, you end up juggling portals and re-keying tickets.

Compliance mattered too. With regulators tightening breach-reporting windows and clients asking for PCI or HIPAA evidence, we favored vendors that package audit-ready reports instead of raw log dumps.

Finally, we checked real-world sentiment. Gartner Peer Insights, G2, and MSP community boards gave us unfiltered customer stories—praise and pain alike. High scores alone weren’t enough; we confirmed those scores came from active MSP deployments, not one-off enterprise deals.

Run through that sieve, only five providers stayed standing. In the next sections, we’ll group them by the business problems they solve so you can jump straight to the option that fits your model.

## Which road fits your MSP?

No two MSPs start from the same spot. Some run a mature security stack and just need a deeper SOC bench. Others are still piecing together core tools and want a distributor that can supply the whole menu.

Take a quick pulse check:

*Do you prefer curating your own blend of best-of-breed tech, with billing and enablement handled in one place?*

If that sounds like you, the aggregator route is the most flexible play. Let’s unpack the standout choice in that camp.

TD Synnex sits at the crossroads of distribution and consulting, curating a catalog that spans more than 100 pre-built security solutions sourced from 50-plus vendors. Its [TD Synnex Cybersecurity solutions](https://www.tdsynnex.com/na/us/advancedsolutions/cybersecurity/) page shows how those building blocks—firewalls, SIEM, MDR, user-awareness training—can be mixed, matched, and then wrapped in enablement programs that help you weave everything into a branded service.

From there, the Managed Service Licence Agreement lets you buy software on a pay-as-you-grow basis, matching the monthly cadence of your MRR model.

Why it matters:

## Need concierge-level MDR and compliance?

Some clients live under a microscope. Public companies racing to meet the SEC’s four-day breach-disclosure rule, healthcare groups bracing for HIPAA audits, and manufacturers chasing cyber-insurance renewals all need proof that someone is watching their network every second and can document the evidence.

That is where high-touch Managed Detection and Response rises to the task. Let’s start with the name most MSPs hear first.

### Arctic Wolf: concierge security for the mid-market

Arctic Wolf built its reputation on one promise: a named security team that feels like part of yours. Each partner receives two dedicated analysts who tune detections, brief executives, and guide incident response. Most rivals reserve that personal touch for Fortune 100 budgets.

![](https://waybar.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/image-2-1024x480.jpeg)

*Arctic Wolf MDR concierge security webpage screenshot.*

Coverage is broad. The platform ingests endpoint, network, cloud, and identity telemetry, then correlates activity inside the company’s cloud-native SOC. Analysts act within minutes when something looks off, isolating hosts or steering remediation so you are not left staring at an alert queue.

The approach resonates. In Gartner’s 2024 Voice of the Customer report, Arctic Wolf earned a 4.8-of-5 overall rating with 98 percent willingness to recommend across 451 reviews.

Why MSPs partner up:

- Channel-first DNA. Arctic Wolf sells through MSPs and VARs, never direct, so incentives align from day one.
- Integration ready. ConnectWise, ServiceNow, and Microsoft 365 hooks route incidents where your techs already work.
- Compliance lift. Pre-built PCI, HIPAA, and ISO-27001 reports shorten audit prep, while 12-month log retention satisfies most cyber-insurance questionnaires.

Pricing lands in the premium tier, often based on user or asset counts plus a one-time onboarding fee. MSPs report they regain margin quickly by avoiding the staffing and shift coverage a home-grown SOC would demand.

If your clients expect board-level assurance and you want to give them a security hotline answered by name, Arctic Wolf delivers concierge protection without the enterprise-only price tag.

### eSentire: premium MDR when regulators sit in the front row

Some networks sit under a bullseye and a binder of rules. SEC-listed firms now have four business days to file breach reports, and regional banks juggle PCI and FFIEC audits in the same quarter. For that level of scrutiny, MSPs rely on eSentire.

eSentire pioneered MDR more than two decades ago, and the experience shows. Its Atlas XDR platform ingests endpoint, network, log, and cloud signals, then routes them to analysts who target a 15-minute mean time to contain. When a threat slips past basic defenses, the SOC can isolate hosts or block IP ranges and deliver a completion report ready for any auditor.

Partners praise the high-touch service. The company posts a 72 Net Promoter Score—rare in security services—and protects more than 2,000 organisations across 80 countries.

Why it clicks:

- Regulator-grade reporting. Quarterly compliance packs map controls to HIPAA, GDPR, PCI, and more, saving hours of spreadsheet work.
- Incident response on tap. An IR retainer with four-hour remote threat suppression folds forensics into the subscription, so surprise bills never hit client budgets.
- Global reach. SOCs in North America and EMEA respect data-sovereignty rules and provide follow-the-sun staffing.

Pricing sits in the premium tier, reflecting the hands-on approach and bundled IR. Clients with deep compliance exposure quickly see ROI; smaller SMBs may not.

If your prospect list is full of auditors and board-level security committees, eSentire supplies both the muscle and the paperwork to satisfy them while you keep the relationship.

## Protect high-volume SMB clients on a budget

## Huntress: enterprise-grade detection for the 99 percent

If your customer base skews toward small retailers, charities, or ten-person law firms, you know the challenge: budgets stay slim while threats keep growing. Huntress exists to close that gap.

The platform deploys in minutes through a lightweight agent. From there, a human SOC team hunts for footholds ransomware actors love—registry run keys, scheduled tasks, suspicious PowerShell, and Microsoft 365 credential abuse. When trouble appears, the team returns a plain-language incident report with one-click remediation. No twenty-page PDF, no cryptic MITRE codes.

Partners highlight two stand-outs:

- Noise reduction. Every alert is human-vetted, so your techs focus on issues they can fix instead of chasing false positives.
- Education culture. Huntress supports the MSP community with free webinars, tabletop exercises, and NFR licences. Your junior tech grows smarter after every incident.

Pricing stays transparent at about five dollars per endpoint per month, with a 50-endpoint minimum for direct licensing. That makes it easy to bake security into even the smallest MSA without scaring prospects.

Limitations? Huntress focuses on endpoints and Microsoft 365 today. If you need deep network or multi-cloud telemetry, you will layer another tool. Still, for the bulk of SMB risk—phishing, ransomware, living-off-the-land attacks—Huntress provides rapid detection, clear guidance, and a budget-friendly entry point.

When you must roll out serious protection across hundreds of small clients tomorrow morning, few options match the speed and clarity Huntress delivers.

## Four trends shaping MSP security strategy in 2026

The managed-security landscape never sits still. Regulations tighten, vendors converge, and insurers rewrite the fine print. Here are the headline shifts influencing which partner you choose, and how you package the service.

![](https://waybar.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/image-5-1024x572.jpeg)

1\. Regulation clocks keep shrinking

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission now gives public companies just four business days to disclose a material cyber incident. That deadline ripples downstream to every MSP supporting listed clients. Providers that bundle forensic help, audit-ready logging, and fast incident-response service-level targets rise to the top because they make that four-day sprint survivable.

2\. XDR momentum is real, not marketing vapor

Customers are done juggling ten consoles. Vendors are responding by fusing endpoint, network, cloud, and identity telemetry into a single data lake, and MDR partners are hitching their engines to that lake. When you evaluate a service, check whether new modules share one detection pipeline or operate as bolt-ons. A true platform reduces overhead and slashes false positives.

3\. AI moves from buzzword to baseline

Every SOC splashes “machine learning” across its slide deck, yet the best outcomes still pair automation with seasoned analysts who spot context that algorithms miss. Use proofs of concept to test triage quality. If a vendor’s AI trims alert volume without extending dwell time, you gain efficiency without losing depth.

4\. Cyber-insurance underwriters shape the roadmap

Policies increasingly demand evidence of 24 × 7 monitoring, documented incident-response playbooks, and multi-factor authentication everywhere. MSPs that tick all three boxes help clients lower premiums, or even qualify for coverage at all. When a provider advertises a breach warranty or insurer partnership, read the clauses; they can become potent sales accelerators.
