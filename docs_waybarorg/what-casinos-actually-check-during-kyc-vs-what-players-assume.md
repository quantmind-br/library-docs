Most of that friction is preventable. The verification requirements aren’t arbitrary. They follow from specific anti-money laundering regulations that licensed operators are legally required to satisfy. Understanding what’s being checked and why makes the process navigable rather than opaque.

## **What’s Actually Being Verified**

**Identity** – The casino needs to confirm you are who you say you are and that you meet the minimum age requirement. A government-issued photo ID satisfies this: passport, national identity card, or driving license. The document must be current – expired IDs are commonly rejected even when the expiry is recent. Both sides are typically required.

**Address** – Proof of residence confirms where you actually live, which affects tax reporting obligations and ensures you’re not located in a restricted jurisdiction. Acceptable documents are usually utility bills, bank statements, or official correspondence dated within 90 days. Documents outside this window are rejected regardless of how recent the address itself is.

**Payment method ownership** – This is the one that surprises most players. The casino must verify that the payment method belongs to the same person whose identity it has verified. For cards, a photo of the card showing the last four digits and the name is standard. For e-wallets, a screenshot of the account showing name and email address. The underlying principle: deposits and withdrawals must flow through accounts that belong to the account holder, closing a basic money laundering route.

## **What Players Assume But Is Wrong**

The most common misconception is that KYC only applies at large withdrawal thresholds. Regulatory requirements increasingly mandate verification before any withdrawal regardless of amount – and some licensed operators must verify before deposits are accepted. Assuming you can play and cash out with minimal verification is a hangover from older, less regulated environments.

The second misconception is that partial documents suffice. A driving license submitted front-only, or a utility bill where the account number is visible but the address is cut off, won’t clear verification. The reviewing team is checking specific fields and will request resubmission rather than guessing at the missing information.
