## FAQs: Houston Car Accident Claims Explained

**1. How quickly must I reach out to an attorney regarding my car crash in Houston?**

You must reach an attorney immediately. When you take rapid action, you preserve tangible evidence and strengthen your claim. An attorney can prepare you to speak with insurers by helping you to understand your rights. By taking this easy step, you can avoid making costly errors. At the very least, a consultation will allow you to determine your rights.

**2. What kinds of compensation can I expect as a result of a car accident?**

As a result of an accident, you have the right to receive payment for your medical expenses, lost income (wages), and damage done to your property. Additionally, you may be entitled to payment for pain and suffering, emotional distress, and diminished earning ability. Your claim will be based upon the individual facts surrounding your case. An attorney will review your medical records and calculate your actual damages, so that an attorney can ensure you do not sustain any significant damages or losses.

**3. What if I am partially responsible for my accident?**

Texas uses the theory of modified comparative fault. Therefore, if you are less than 50% at fault for the accident, you may recover damages. The damages you do receive will be reduced by your percentage of fault. An attorney can help you to minimize your unreasonable fault. Having objective evidence and a persuasive argument makes a great deal of difference.

**4. How long will it take to resolve my claim after an automobile accident in Houston?**

Some claims are resolved within a few months of the date of the accident. However, other claims take much longer, particularly when there are significant injuries involved. The time frame for resolving a claim depends on medical treatment; the amount of evidence compiled; and the response from the insurer. A competent and experienced legal team is able to expedite the resolution of claims without compromising your long-term interest.

**5. Do most automobile accident cases go to trial?**

Most cases are settled prior to going to trial. However, in general, by preparing as though your case will eventually proceed to trial is generally beneficial to improving the settlement offer you receive. Insurance carriers are generally more responsive to law firms who have shown they are prepared to litigate. Therefore, being adequately prepared for trial will likely lead to favorable results, even though you ultimately never go to trial.
