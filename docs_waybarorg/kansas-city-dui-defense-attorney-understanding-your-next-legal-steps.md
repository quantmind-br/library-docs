## So, What Does a DUI Charge Really Mean?

DUI is frequently referred to as DWI (Driving While Intoxicated) in Missouri. The same concept. If your blood alcohol content is 0.08% or greater, you are prohibited from driving by law. The trick is that officers don’t always require that number. On the basis of your actions alone, they may arrest you. slurred words. Unbalanced. The fragrance of alcohol. Does it seem unfair? A lot of people believe that. And that’s the role of defense.

## Right After the Arrest—Don’t Panic, But Don’t Wait Either

This part is critical. After a DUI arrest, two things happen at once:

- A criminal case begins
- Your driver’s license is at risk

Most people focus only on the court case. That’s a mistake. You have **15 days** to request a hearing to keep your license. Miss that window, and your license could be suspended automatically. That’s not a long time. Life gets busy. People forget. But this deadline doesn’t matter. You know what? ## The Legal Process (It’s Not as Confusing as It Seems)

Let’s break it down simply.

### 1. Booking and Release

You’re taken in, processed, then released—often within hours.

### 2. Arraignment

This is your first court appearance. You hear the charges and enter a plea.

### 3. Pre-Trial Motions

Your lawyer reviews evidence. This may include:

- Breath test results
- Police reports
- Body cam footage

If something looks off, your attorney can challenge it.

### 4. Negotiation or Trial

Some cases settle. Others go to trial. It depends on the facts—and how strong your defense is. It sounds formal. And it is. But at its core, it’s about one thing: proving whether the state’s case holds up.

## Defense Strategies—Because Not Every Case Is Clear-Cut

Here’s the thing—DUI cases aren’t always airtight. There are often gaps. Mistakes. Assumptions. A solid defense may focus on:

### Faulty Traffic Stop

Police must have a valid reason to stop you. No reason? That’s a problem for the prosecution.

### Breathalyzer Issues

Machines fail. Calibration errors happen. Even small issues can affect results.

### Field Sobriety Test Problems

These tests depend on judgment. Weather, nerves, or health can impact performance.

### Medical Conditions

Some conditions mimic signs of intoxication. It happens more than people think.

### Chain of Evidence

If evidence wasn’t handled properly, it may not hold up in court. Each case is different. No one-size-fits-all defense exists.

## What About Penalties? Let’s Be Real

DUI penalties vary. But here’s a rough idea:

- First offense: fines, license suspension, possible jail time
- Repeat offenses: higher fines, longer suspensions, mandatory programs

It adds up quickly—financially and personally. Insurance rates rise. Job issues may come up. Even travel can get tricky. That’s why early action matters.

**1. After a DUI arrest, what should I do right away?**

Act quickly. Within 15 days, request a hearing for a license. Next, speak with a DUI lawyer. You can safeguard your driving privileges and develop your case early on, before the evidence becomes more difficult to refute.

**2. In Missouri, am I able to decline a breathalyzer test?**

There is a catch, though. Automatic license suspension may result from refusal. Depending on the circumstances, it might still support your defense. What pertains to you can be clarified by a lawyer.

**3. Will I always have a DUI on my record?**

For years, a DUI can remain on your record. After a waiting time, certain instances in Missouri can be eligible for expungement. However, not every situation is eligible. Here, legal advice is crucial.

**4. For a first offense, is a DUI attorney really necessary?**

Sure. Serious consequences follow from a first transgression. A lawyer may defend your license, lower charges, or contest the evidence. You have fewer options when you go alone.
