You’ve probably heard the jokes, seen the commercials, and maybe even experienced the discomfort yourself. Hemorrhoids are one of those conditions that affect millions of people yet somehow remain shrouded in embarrassment and misinformation. Let’s clear the air and talk frankly about what’s really going on down there.

## What Exactly Are Hemorrhoids?

Think of hemorrhoids as swollen veins in your lower rectum and anus. They’re actually normal structures that everyone has, believe it or not. The problem occurs when they become inflamed or enlarged. You’ve got two types: internal hemorrhoids that form inside the rectum, and external ones that develop under the skin around your anus. Internal hemorrhoids usually don’t hurt because there aren’t many pain receptors up there, but they can bleed. External hemorrhoids? Those are the painful ones you’ll definitely notice.

## When Night Falls, The Itching Calls

Here’s something you might have noticed: the itching seems worse when you’re trying to sleep. There’s actually science behind [why hemorrhoids itch more at night](https://www.doctronic.ai/blog/why-do-hemorrhoids-itch-more-at-night/). Your body’s cortisol levels drop in the evening, which means less natural anti-inflammatory response. You’re also lying down, which can increase blood flow to the area. Plus, you’re finally still and paying attention to your body instead of being distracted by daytime activities. The combination creates a perfect storm of awareness and actual increased irritation.

## What Causes These Unwelcome Visitors?

Straining during bowel movements tops the list. When you push too hard, you’re putting serious pressure on those veins. Pregnancy is another major culprit because of the extra weight and pressure on your pelvic area. Sitting for long periods, whether at your desk or on the toilet, doesn’t help either.

Other contributing factors include:

- Chronic constipation or diarrhea
- Obesity
- Low-fiber diet
- Heavy lifting
- Aging (the tissues supporting these veins weaken over time)

## Signs You’re Dealing with Hemorrhoids

Bright red blood on your toilet paper or in the bowl is often the first sign. You might feel a tender lump near your anus. Itching and irritation in the anal region are common complaints. Pain or discomfort, especially when sitting, can range from mild to make-you-wince severe. Some people experience a feeling of fullness or incomplete evacuation after bowel movements.

## Getting Relief Without the Drama

The good news? Most hemorrhoids respond well to home treatment. Increase your fiber intake to soften stools and reduce straining. Drink plenty of water throughout the day. Take warm sitz baths for 10 to 15 minutes, especially after bowel movements. Over-the-counter creams and suppositories can provide temporary relief from itching and pain.

Don’t spend excessive time on the toilet. Get in, do your business, get out. Your phone can wait.

Most hemorrhoids clear up within a week or two with home care. However, you should definitely see your doctor if you’re experiencing persistent bleeding, severe pain, or if symptoms don’t improve after a week of home treatment. Rectal bleeding can also signal more serious conditions, so it’s worth getting checked out.

## The Reality Check

Hemorrhoids are incredibly common, affecting about half of adults by age 50. They’re treatable, manageable, and nothing to be ashamed about. Sure, they’re uncomfortable and inconvenient, but with proper care and lifestyle adjustments, you can minimize flare-ups and get back to comfortable living. Your bottom deserves better, so don’t suffer in silence.
